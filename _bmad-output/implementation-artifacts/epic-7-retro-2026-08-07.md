## What went well

- **The staged de-risking worked exactly as designed.** Inserting a **read-only pre-flight (7.6)** before the real-money gate (7.7) caught **two order-blocking bugs before a dollar moved** — had we gone straight to a real order, we'd have hit them mid-trade.
- **7.2's generalized atomic-claim primitive + fixed-point money path** meant the real placement had no double-order risk and serialized the $29.895 fill exactly.
- **Test discipline held** — every fix (token wrap/unwrap) landed with a regression test; suite stayed green (631).
- **7.6's honesty in scope** — it explicitly declared seam 6 (order-status/fill) out of read-only scope and deferred it to 7.7, so nothing was silently claimed as "confirmed."

## Challenges & lessons (systems, not people)

1. **schwab-py 1.5.x metadata-wraps the token** (`{creation_timestamp, token:{…}}`) on **both** the client-read (`client_from_access_functions`) and exchange-write callback. Our code assumed the bare oauth token both ways → first "token format has changed," then (after fixing read) empty tokens → Schwab `token_invalid`. **Fixed both sides (commit 424864a).**
   - *Lesson:* a live third-party SDK's token/contract shape is a guess until a real payload proves it — the pre-flight is the right place to find that out.
2. **Callback-URL mismatch** (`.env` `:7080/api/schwab/callback` vs portal `/callback`) surfaced as a Schwab `4001 assertion_rejected` that masqueraded as an app/account problem — and was initially mis-attributed away from the callback.
   - *Lesson:* on an ambiguous auth failure, **verify config exact-match (callback, app key) early** — it's cheap and it's the most common cause; don't over-anchor on one theory.
3. **Schwab auth code is ~30s and single-use** — it kept expiring across the human→assistant→exchange round-trip.
   - *Lesson:* for time-boxed secrets, keep the exchange **local and immediate** (a self-serve terminal script beat the round-trip).
