## 2. What went well

**1. The "structural teeth" pattern held across all 10 stories — this is the epic's signature.**
Every trust invariant was made *physically un-bypassable at a code seam*, not just checked:
- 4.1 — a schema-less LLM call is un-issuable through the gateway (the port rejects it before any model call).
- 4.2 — `BlessedRecommendation` can only be constructed through the gate via a module-private sentinel checked in `__post_init__`.
- 4.6 — `coach.execution.execute_approved_order` is the *sole* caller of `place_order`, locked by a grep canary.
- 4.9 — the Coach Engine is the *sole* writer of decision records (AD-6), one-shot co-sign guarded on `status=="proposed"`.

Trust invariants enforced by construction, not by convention-plus-hope. This is why review found zero high-severity issues in ten stories.

**2. "Never a dead-end / never a block" (FR7) delivered deterministically and testably offline.**
The default-plan fallback guarantees the coach always surfaces something backed and honest, and the fake gateway's non-matching evidence IDs make that fallback the *default* offline path — so the never-a-dead-end guarantee is fully exercised without network or credentials. Every story ran zero-network, zero-credential.

**3. Hexagonal port/adapter discipline, mirrored from existing modules.**
The LLM gateway (4.1) and broker execution (4.6/4.7) both followed the established `marketdata/` pattern: a fake as the tested default plus a credential-gated *raising* stub for the real adapter. Reusing the known shape kept review noise down and consistency high.

**4. Product-voice honesty was enforced down to the copy.**
Multiple patches existed purely to keep the promise honest: FR14 uncertainties reworded so they never smuggle a favorable benefit claim (4.4, 4.5); truthful serialization of non-FILLED order statuses at HTTP 200 rather than faking a fill (4.7); a distinct calm "couldn't load" state separated from the truly-empty invite so a user with history is never told "no decisions yet" (4.10); the co-sign heading gated so it never appears without co-sign data (4.10). The "calm, honest, never-red" UX rules were treated as real acceptance criteria.

**5. Scope discipline was mature.**
High reject counts with clear, documented reasoning; consistent, correct hand-offs between stories ("that's 4.6's concern," "the gate was frozen in 4.3"). The autonomous loop understood the seam boundaries and didn't scope-creep.

## 3. What was hard — and the one systemic finding

**⭐ SYSTEMIC FINDING #1 — Concurrency/atomicity is the recurring blind spot, and it sits exactly where it's most dangerous.**
The same class of defect was deferred twice, at the two most sensitive seams in the whole product:
- **4.9** — concurrent in-flight double-`/approve` of the same `decision_id` is not atomically guarded (read-status → place → write-cosign is not one transaction; the idempotency key is minted per-request, not persisted per-decision; no row lock / conditional update / unique index). Against a *real* broker this could place two real orders.
- **5.1** (next epic, already shipped) — the weekly-digest job has the identical shape: the "never double-send" claim only holds for strictly-sequential re-runs. A duplicate proactive email directly violates the core "never nag" promise (NFR8).

**Root cause:** the project's chosen convention is *sole-writer + grep canary + `create_all`, with no DB triggers, CHECK constraints, unique indexes, or row locks*. That convention is clean and it worked beautifully for **identity** invariants (only-X-may-write). But it structurally *cannot express an atomic claim* ("win the race to move this record from proposed→placing"). It's harmless in single-user offline v1 — and it is the #1 thing to fix before any real money or any real proactive email goes out.

**SYSTEMIC FINDING #2 — The entire epic's happy path is proven only against fakes.**
Every "done" story ships the real Anthropic gateway and the real Schwab `place_order`/`get_order_status` as credential-gated *raising stubs*. This was the correct, disciplined v1 choice — but it means **"Epic 4 done" ≠ "the Coach works against a live LLM and a live broker."** A real, coherent body of integration risk (SDK install, live structured-output behavior, real order placement + reconciliation mapping, live-session provider match under real tokens) has been *deferred, not retired*. It should be planned explicitly, not discovered later.

**Smaller recurring frictions:**
- **Money serialization drift.** The `E+` exponent-notation bug was born in 4.6, flagged-and-deferred in 4.7, and only closed on the `/approve` path in 4.9 — three stories to fully corner a formatting decision made once at a seam. A shared money-formatter from day one would have prevented it.
- **`uv.lock` anthropic-drift toil.** Reviewers' `uv run` kept re-adding `anthropic` and dirtying the tree (had to be reverted in 4.4, 4.5). Known gotcha, still costing cycles.

## 5. Readiness assessment — is Epic 4 *really* done?

- **Code-complete against fakes:** ✅ Yes. 264 tests green, zero regressions, every trust invariant structurally locked.
- **Deployed:** ❌ No.
- **Integration-tested against real LLM + real broker:** ❌ No — deliberately deferred (raising stubs).
- **Safe to move real money:** ❌ Not yet — the concurrency atomicity gap (Finding #1) must land first.

**Verdict:** Epic 4 is *complete and excellent as a v1 offline milestone*. It is **not** production-ready against real money, and the honest framing is "the Coach is fully built and proven against fakes; wiring it to reality is a distinct, deferred body of work."

## 7. Key takeaways

1. **The structural-teeth pattern is the project's greatest strength** — keep making invariants un-bypassable at a seam. It bought ten stories with zero high-severity findings.
2. **The convention has one gap: it enforces identity, not atomicity.** Introduce a real atomic-claim primitive (DB lock / conditional update / unique index) *before* real money or real proactive email — the same root cause already surfaced twice (4.9, 5.1).
3. **"Done against fakes" is a real milestone but not production-readiness.** The live LLM + live broker integration is a deferred cliff, not retired risk — plan it as explicit scope.
4. **Small seam decisions propagate.** The money-format drift took three stories to close; a shared helper from day one prevents the whole class.
